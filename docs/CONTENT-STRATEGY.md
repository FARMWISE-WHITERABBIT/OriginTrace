# OriginTrace Content Strategy — EU-Bound African Exporters + EU/Dubai Importers

_Prepared 2026-07-09. Grounded in: Search Console data (July 2026), the existing 20-post blog inventory, and two verified research sweeps (exporter-side Nigeria→EU; importer-side EU + UAE) with primary-source citations. Companion to docs/FRICTION-AUDIT.md._

## 1. Strategic frame — what the evidence says

1. **Commodity-specific compliance content is what ranks for us.** The GACC registration guide alone pulls 34k impressions. Generic "traceability" thought-leadership pulls hundreds. Every new topic below is {commodity} × {market} × {regulation/process} specific.
2. **EUDR was delayed a second time** (Reg (EU) 2025/2650, OJ 23 Dec 2025): large/medium operators comply from **30 Dec 2026**, micro/small from **30 June 2027**. DDS is filed only by the first placer on the EU market; annual DDS filing is now possible; "micro/small primary operator" relief (postal address instead of GPS) applies **only in low-risk countries — so not Nigeria**. July 2026 = the final 6-month preparation window. All content framing follows from this.
3. **Sesame and ginger are NOT EUDR commodities** (Annex I: cattle, cocoa, coffee, oil palm, rubber, soya, wood). Their real EU pain: Nigerian sesame is under **Reg 2019/1793 increased controls — 50% Salmonella checks + per-consignment ISO-17025 lab report + official certificate**; ginger's pain is MRLs, **aflatoxin B1 ≤ 5 µg/kg**, and a new **Ralstonia additional declaration** on the phytosanitary certificate. Nobody serves these queries for Nigeria — it's the widest-open gap found.
4. **EUDR country benchmarking asymmetry is an unexploited story:** Ghana = low risk, Nigeria = standard risk (CIR 2025/1093). Consequences (1% vs 3% checks, simplified vs full due diligence, MSPO relief availability) are explained by no incumbent.
5. **The content competitor is TraceX** (plus Coolset, Meridia, Live-EO, osapiens) — programmatic, importer-tilted, imprecise on Nigerian institutional reality (NEPC, NXP, NAFDAC, FMARD, NCMC). **Our differentiation: local institutional precision + exact regulation citations.** B2B directories (Tridge/Volza) own sourcing queries but can't write compliance depth.
6. **Cocoa price collapse reframes the pitch:** with farmgate prices cut 28–57% (Ghana/CIV, early 2026), the angle is no longer "capture the premium" but "EUDR-readiness is how you KEEP the EU channel when prices crater."

## 4. Conversion architecture (traffic → demo)

- **Persona-split CTAs, real endpoints (docs/IMPORTER-FUNNEL-PLAN.md, P1).** Exporters: *"Get your shipment EU-ready — see your readiness score"* → `/demo` (pre-shipment scoring). Importers/buyers (EU/UK/USA/China/UAE — not just EU): *"Verify a supplier before you commit — request a supplier risk snapshot"* → `/demo?role=buyer`, **never the bare `/demo`** (registry status + plot geodata + deforestation overlay + inspection history; research shows buyers currently stitch this from NEPC lookups + directories + inspection firms with no integrated option — our strongest converting promise). UAE importers: *"One data pack for Dubai, the GCC, and the EU."* → `/demo?role=buyer`. Replace generic bottom-CTAs with these; place one mid-article.
- **The importer end-CTA is the onboarding play, and it routes to `/importers`, not `/demo`.** Self-serve buyer registration doesn't exist (`app/auth/buyer-register` redirects to `/auth/login`; buyer orgs are superadmin-provisioned) — so the end CTA can't promise instant signup. Verification content hooks the buyer, but the product motion is: the importer joins OriginTrace, onboards their exporters, and mandates on-platform documentation as a condition of the order — supply chain, compliance evidence, lab tests, documents, and shipment tracking maintained at origin and visible from the buyer workspace. Every importer-facing post ends on "bring your suppliers onto OriginTrace" → `/importers`, with the risk-snapshot ask as the mid-article step → `/demo?role=buyer`. (Escrow milestone automation — payment releases triggered by verified shipping events — is built; see docs/ESCROW-SHIPPING-APIS.md. Escrow *narrative* — what to actually say about it — follows docs/IMPORTER-FUNNEL-PLAN.md §3: never "LC-grade" or credit-substitution language, lead with cost/friction vs. an LC's paperwork, not payment-speed superiority.)
- **Lead magnets (gated, feed demo pipeline):** (1) Nigeria→EU sesame document checklist; (2) EUDR importer due-diligence checklist (D5); (3) annotated "what a compliant DDS package looks like" specimen; (4) supplier verification template; (5) UAE FIRS document-pack checklist; (6) consignment-rejection cost calculator (interactive — we already build calculators, cf. compliance-calculator.tsx).
- **Hub-and-spoke internal linking.** Every cluster post links early (not just bottom-CTA) to its money page; money pages link back to the 2–3 best cluster posts. The GACC lesson: 34k impressions with one buried CTA converted almost nothing.
- **Trust = citation precision.** Research shows the vendor-blog competition rarely cites regulation numbers precisely. We cite CELEX/regulation numbers, dates, and annexes — and re-verify the flagged-unverified items before publishing (list in §5).
- **Technical:** FAQ schema on every post (component exists, unused); keep test assertions structural (`data-testid`), never copy-based; bare `metadata.title` (layout adds the suffix); every post ships through the `marketing-page` + `vercel-preflight` skills.
- **Measurement:** GSC by cluster monthly (impressions → CTR → clicks), demo-request attribution by landing path, lead-magnet downloads. Kill/iterate rule: any P0 post with <1% CTR at >1k impressions after 8 weeks gets a title/description rework (the GACC playbook).

## 6. Geographic reality check (GSC country data, July 2026)

What the country breakdown shows, and how it re-weights the plan:

- **Most impressions are off-ICP bycatch.** The US alone is 25,701 impressions / 8 clicks (0.03% CTR); Brazil 1,726 / 0; LatAm (AR, MX, CO, PE, EC, CL) another ~1,500 / 0. This is the GACC guide ranking globally for generic "GACC registration" queries — US/Brazilian food exporters to China, not African-commodity ICP. **Consequence: global CTR is a misleading KPI.** Judge CTR *per target geo*, not blended; the "0.24% CTR problem" is substantially an off-market-impressions artifact.
- **Target geos convert well but barely see us.** Nigeria: 21 clicks / 275 impressions (**7.6% CTR** — best in the table) but tiny volume: Google rarely shows us for Nigerian queries because the Nigeria-intent content (NXP/NEPC, sesame/ginger export process) doesn't exist yet. Clusters A/B/C attack exactly this — the constraint is impressions, not CTR.
- **EU importer audience is present and under-converted:** UK 10/729, NL 7/774, DE 3/720, ES 3/330, IT 2/378, FR 1/402. Cluster D content + title/description work is the lever; NL/DE/UK ports match the sesame/cocoa entry-point story.
- **UAE is nearly invisible** (2/137) — Cluster E builds that audience from scratch; expect to create impressions, not harvest them.
- **Origin-expansion signal:** Ghana 3/61, Côte d'Ivoire 2/30, Kenya 3/222, Ethiopia 2/74, Tanzania 2/63 — small but present. C2 (Ghana low-risk benchmarking) is the natural first pull for Ghana; an East-Africa variant (Kenya/Ethiopia sesame + coffee-EUDR) is a later wave if these grow.
- **India (16/740) is the #2 clicker** — Indian sesame/spice exporters hitting the same compliance content. Not ICP; treat as bycatch, don't invest, but it validates that commodity-compliance queries travel.

**Measurement change:** report the funnel as {cluster × target-geo} — e.g. Cluster A success = Nigeria impressions growth + Nigeria CTR ≥ 5%; Cluster D success = UK/NL/DE clicks; Cluster E = UAE impressions from ~zero. Demo-request attribution stays the north star.
